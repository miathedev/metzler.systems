I run Kubernetes in production — and everything underneath it: the Linux hosts, the observability stack, the CI/CD, the network, and the security hardening that keeps it all boring.

**The short version:** I'm a DevOps and Site Reliability Engineer with roughly three years of Kubernetes in production, a Linux background since childhood, and a habit of building platforms that fail over instead of failing. I hold the KCNA certification, am completing the CKA this year, and I'm working toward the full CNCF Kubestronaut set. I'm open to full-time or contract roles in the DE/CH border region (Küssaberg, DE, 5 minutes from Bad Zurzach, G permit DE→CH).

## The long version

**hmmh multimediahaus AG, Bremen (2018 – 2019).**\
Where it started: keeping Jenkins alive and honest. I parallelised CI pipelines, migrated JUnit4 to JUnit5, and fixed bugs in a production Java/Spring e-commerce platform. Lesson learned: CI/CD is not a tool, it's the heartbeat of a team.

**engram GmbH, Bremen (2019 – 2023).**\
DevOps and Embedded Systems. I migrated branch display systems (digital signage, customer guidance) from Windows to Linux — replacing costly Windows licences — and owned CI/CD pipelines, QA workflows, and deployment strategies for display hardware and cloud environments. Lesson learned: migrating real hardware estates teaches you more about infrastructure than any lab.

**Leo Skull GmbH, Oldenburg (since 2023 — freelance cluster operation since April 2024).**\
The migration: I moved the production infrastructure from Google Cloud to self-hosted Kubernetes — with HA SaaS environments, automated failover, load balancing, and Prometheus/Grafana/Loki monitoring. Since April 2024 I freelance-operate exactly this cluster: patching, consulting, support. Lesson learned: the cloud bill is a design review you didn't know you were doing.

**bpm consult ag, Hägendorf SO (since 2024).**\
Platform ownership behind beeline — 50+ online shops on Linux, KVM and InterSystems IRIS. I built the observability stack (Prometheus/Mimir, Loki, Alertmanager, OpenTelemetry, IPMI) with data-driven root-cause analysis, a highly available active/active container platform, an HA reverse-proxy cluster against bot and DDoS traffic, internal PKI with an offline root CA, HashiCorp Vault, hardware-key MFA, and the 2026 technology budget. Lesson learned: security hardening and user experience are the same project.

## What I care about

- Minimal moving parts. Every daemon needs a reason to exist.
- Observability that answers "why did it break" — not "something is broken".
- Automation that a colleague can read six months later.
- Backups that have actually been restored once.
