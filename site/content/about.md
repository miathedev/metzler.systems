I run Kubernetes in production, and everything underneath it: the Linux hosts, the observability stack, the CI/CD, the network, and the security hardening that keeps it all boring.

I'm a DevOps and Site Reliability Engineer with three years of Kubernetes in production. Linux since childhood. I like platforms that fail over instead of failing. KCNA certified, CKA this year, Kubestronaut next. Open to full-time or contract work in the DE/CH border region (Küssaberg, DE, five minutes from Bad Zurzach, G permit DE->CH).

## The long version

hmmh multimediahaus AG, Bremen (2018-2019).\
Where it started: keeping Jenkins alive. I parallelised CI pipelines, migrated JUnit4 to JUnit5, and fixed bugs in a production Java/Spring e-commerce platform. CI/CD turned out to be the heartbeat of a team.

engram GmbH, Bremen (2019-2023).\
DevOps and Embedded Systems. I moved branch display systems (digital signage, customer guidance) from Windows to Linux, which also killed the licence costs, and owned CI/CD, QA and deployments for display hardware and cloud. Real hardware estates teach you more than any lab.

Leo Skull GmbH, Oldenburg (since 2023, freelance cluster operation since April 2024).\
The migration: I moved production from Google Cloud to self-hosted Kubernetes, HA SaaS environments with automated failover, Prometheus/Grafana/Loki on top. Since April 2024 I freelance-operate that same cluster: patching, consulting, support. The cloud bill, it turns out, is a design review you didn't know you were doing.

bpm consult ag, Hägendorf SO (since 2024).\
Platform ownership behind beeline, 50+ online shops on Linux, KVM and InterSystems IRIS. I built the observability stack (Prometheus/Mimir, Loki, Alertmanager, OpenTelemetry, IPMI) with data-driven root-cause analysis, an active/active container platform, an HA reverse-proxy cluster against bot and DDoS traffic, internal PKI with an offline root CA, HashiCorp Vault, hardware-key MFA, and the 2026 technology budget. Security hardening and user experience turned out to be the same project.

## What I care about

- Minimal moving parts. Every daemon needs a reason to exist.
- Observability that answers "why did it break", not "something is broken".
- Automation that a colleague can read six months later.
- Backups that have actually been restored once.
